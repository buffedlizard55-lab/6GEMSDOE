“Keep the metric-aware placement step (spacing predictions roughly 4-5 pixels apart to match the 300 m kernel radius) — this is correct geometry, not a shortcut.”task brief (no source given)Measured false. Scored on the same blocked folds, 4-5 px thinning (skeleton_spaced4) is the worst non-trivial strategy (0.0621), while binarising the probability surface at the CV-optimal threshold is the best (0.1119) — denser, not sparser. The algebra agrees: a 300 m kernel does not imply 4-5 px spacing, because a missed ground-truth pixel costs beta = 0.8 while a neighbouring prediction at 2 px collects only k = 1/3 of its credit. See the cross-validation table.
The provided magnetic/gravity derivative bands make the brief's requested edge products redundant or missing.measured: scripts/analysis.py --only bandstmi_hg is the horizontal gradient magnitude of TMI (r = 1.000 vs the computed |∇TMI|, r = -0.03 vs dT/dx) — so the analytic signal built from it is valid. But the gravity horizontal-gradient band is not |∇(isostatic gravity)| (r = 0.03): the computed gravity HGM carries new information. det_elev_slope is close to the computed slope (r = 0.95), i.e. largely redundant. tc matched neither the tilt angle (r = 0.01) nor the Laplacian (r = -0.00) and is used as an opaque provided band rather than guessed at.
The reference solution uses spatially blocked cross-validation.https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solutionIt does not. It splits patches with np.random.default_rng permutation — a random split that leaks along faults. We use blocked, buffered folds instead.
